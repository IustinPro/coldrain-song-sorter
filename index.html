    coldrain (stylised in lowercase) are a Japanese rock band from Nagoya, formed in 2007. Their music pairs melodic singing with the screamed vocals of post-hardcore and metalcore, and although the band are Japanese, every song they have released is written in English.

    The lineup has not changed since the day the band formed: vocalist Masato Hayakawa, guitarists Ryo Yokochi (Y.K.C.) and Kazuya Sugiyama (Sugi), bassist Ryo Shimizu (RxYxO) and drummer Katsuma Minatani. Hayakawa has a Japanese father and an American mother, and is fluent in both languages.

    Their first two albums, Final Destination (2009) and The Enemy Inside (2011), were released only in Japan. The band's worldwide debut came in 2014, when Hopeless Records reissued their third album, The Revelation, a year after VAP had released it at home. Vena (2015), Fateless (2017), The Side Effects (2019) and Nonnegative (2022) followed. In 2025 they signed worldwide with Century Media Records, and their eighth album, OPTIMIZE = OPTDEMISE, is due on 25 September 2026.

    Their best known songs include "Mayday", "Gone", "Coexist", "Paradise (Kill the Silence)", "Vengeance", "Die Tomorrow", "No Escape", "The Revelation", "Envy" and "Feed the Fire". Several reached a wider audience as anime and video game themes, and since 2020 the band have hosted their own festival, Blare Fest, in Nagoya.

    History

    2007 – 2008: Formation and major label debut

    coldrain grew out of two Nagoya bands that were rivals on the local circuit and both near their end: Wheel of Life, with RxYxO, Sugi and Y.K.C., and AVER, the band Masato and Katsuma started after their high school group, Script of Creation, split up. What brought them together was taste. The first time the two bands shared a bill, AVER heard Wheel of Life running a Sevendust song in rehearsal, and an excited Katsuma went over to RxYxO: "Don't tell me you're a fan of Sevendust as well!?" After that, every time they met, the talk turned to starting a band together.

    coldrain formed on 17 April 2007, with Masato on vocals, Y.K.C. and Sugi on guitar, Katsuma on drums, and RxYxO, until then a singer, moving to bass for the new group. They found their first fans the hard way, playing school gyms and local clubs in their hometown and handing out demo CDs after every show.

    It's weird in Japan because you have to pay to play gigs, and it kills a lot of artists. You try to find ways around it, like playing in the street and giving out demos. We'd go to [a] ton of shows and give our demos to people outside the venue.

    Masato, to Louder3

    About a year after forming, the band signed a major label deal with VAP. Their debut maxi-single, "Fiction", came out on 5 November 2008, and they took it on their first nationwide tour, thirty venues across Japan.

    2009 – 2011: Final Destination and The Enemy Inside

    A second maxi-single, "8AM", followed the tour. Its limited edition came with a DVD of the title track's video and live footage of "Fiction", "Painting" and "Come Awake", and the song became the ending theme of the anime Hajime no Ippo: New Challenger, the band's first tie-in of any kind. That summer the band made their first appearance at Summer Sonic, and in October 2009 they released their debut album, Final Destination. It peaked at number 88 on the Oricon chart, and the tour behind it sold out.

    Their first EP, Nothing Lasts Forever, was released on 23 June 2010. "We're Not Alone" became the opening theme of the anime Rainbow: Nisha Rokubō no Shichinin, and "Die Tomorrow" was picked for the Pro Evolution Soccer 2011 soundtrack. The band spent the year on the road with acts including Mucc, Avengers in Sci-Fi and Totalfat.

    The second album, The Enemy Inside, arrived on 16 February 2011 and climbed to number 21. It kept the melodic vocals and breakdowns of the earlier songs, and SiM's vocalist MAH sang on "The Maze". That December the band released their first DVD, Three Days of Adrenaline, filmed in Osaka, Nagoya and Tokyo: nineteen live tracks, including all ten from the album, plus behind the scenes footage and the videos for "To Be Alive" and "Rescue Me".

    2012 – 2013: The Revelation

    "No Escape" was released on 20 April 2012 and used in the official Japanese trailer for Resident Evil: Operation Raccoon City. It led the EP Through Clarity on 4 July, the first thing the band had recorded outside Japan, made in the United States with producer David Bendeth, known for his records with Paramore, All Time Low and A Day to Remember.

    On 1 August the band announced that Katsuma was stepping away to be treated for a chronic hereditary illness. Three drummers covered for him: ZAX of Pay Money to My Pain, Tatsu of Crossfaith, and YOUTH-K!!! of BPM13GROOVE. coldrain still played Summer Sonic that summer, and Katsuma came back on 4 November 2012, at a show with Maximum the Hormone at Hiroshima University of Economics.

    From December to February the band were back in the studio with Bendeth. In March 2013 they played Megaport Festival in Kaohsiung, Taiwan, to around five thousand people, and on 17 April they released their third album, The Revelation. It reached number 7 on the Oricon chart, still their highest placing. The Revelation Tour opened in Chiba on 5 May, in the same venue where the band played Ozzfest Japan a week later. Synth lines and strings appeared in the arrangements for the first time, and Bendeth pulled the whole record somewhere heavier in mood.

    David tried to make the record sound darker. He said, after listening to the tracks and after listening to the lyrics, he always aimed for us to sound darker. So it turned out a little darker than what we originally went for and I think it sounds cool.

    Masato, to Mixdown6

    2014: Worldwide debut

    In December 2013 coldrain signed with Raw Power Management, the British company behind Bullet for My Valentine, Bring Me the Horizon, Of Mice & Men and Crossfaith. It put them on Bullet for My Valentine's European tour with Chiodos in February and March 2014, with UK headline dates of their own. Through Clarity got a European release on 27 January, and their second DVD, Evolve, filmed at a one-man show on 18 January, followed on 30 April, their first release on Blu-ray.

    On 9 April the band announced a deal with Hopeless Records to release The Revelation worldwide, outside Asia, Australia and New Zealand, and Hopeless put out the title track the same day. Masato's statement:

    It's hard to put in words how excited and proud we are that The Revelation will be released Worldwide. The dream we've had ever since we started playing music is finally coming true. I can't wait for everyone to hear the songs, learn the words and sing them with us live. We are grateful for the many fans in our homeland that have supported us in becoming the band we are today. Without them this would have been impossible. This step will be the biggest in our careers and we couldn't be more ready.

    Masato, April 20141

    The international edition came out on 23 June in Europe and 24 June in North America, with Sony Music Australia following on 8 August. Five of its new tracks came from Until the End, a six-song EP released in Japan on 18 June. That same month the band played Rock am Ring, Rock im Park and Download Festival, and they were booked for Australia's Soundwave the following year.

    2015 – 2016: Vena

    In June 2015 coldrain played Lunatic Fest, hosted by Luna Sea at Makuhari Messe, on a bill with X Japan, Dir En Grey and Siam Shade. "Words of the Youth" and "Gone" came out in August and September ahead of the fourth album, Vena, released on 21 October in Japan and two days later everywhere else. It was the first coldrain album to go worldwide on day one, reaching number 9 in Japan and number 15 on the UK Independent Breakers chart. Papa Roach's Jacoby Shaddix sang on "Runaway", an offer that arrived by text.

    We grew up listening to P-Roach. We got to tour with them and so far everything about our relationship with them has been a dream come true. One day Jacoby texted me to say, 'If you ever need a guest vocalist...' and I don't know if he was joking or not, but I took that text seriously and took advantage of it!

    Masato, to Louder5

    In the early hours of 20 March 2016, while supporting Silverstein in the United States, the band's bus crashed on the way from Texas to Oklahoma. Nobody was hurt, but with no way of getting there they had to cancel the Tulsa show. A European headline run with Wage War, The Charm the Fury and Counting Days followed in May, from Paris on the 9th to Reading on the 27th, finishing at Slam Dunk Festival. That summer they played the whole of Vans Warped Tour, and on 17 August they released the maxi-single Vena II, with two new songs, "Born to Bleed" and "Undertow", and acoustic takes on "Gone" and "The Story".

    2017 – 2018: Fateless

    The band marked ten years with Fateless, announced on 26 July 2017 and released worldwide on 11 October. The limited edition carried a live CD of their anniversary shows in Nagoya, "Feed the Fire" became the opening theme of King's Game The Animation, and the album peaked at number 8. The Fateless Japan Tour began on 29 October, took in fifteen venues, and ended on 6 February 2018 at the Nippon Budokan. Filmed by Inni Vision, who had directed their videos before, the show was released that September as 20180206 Live at Budokan.

    That autumn coldrain opened for Crown the Empire across Europe alongside Volumes, from Brighton on 18 September to Amsterdam on 15 October. They also wrote "Revolution", the main theme for Mobile Suit Gundam: Extreme Vs. 2, released as a single on 12 December with a video following in January 2019. Days later Masato announced the band were going back into the studio.

    2019 – 2021: The Side Effects

    In June 2019 the band toured Europe, playing Rock am Ring and Rock im Park and closing on the Dogtooth Stage at Download. "Coexist" came out on 4 July with the fastest-growing video they had released, and The Side Effects followed on 28 August, their first album since The Revelation not to arrive in October. It reached number 10. Masato asked for "January 1st", a ballad that was deeply personal to him, to get a video of its own, even though a song like that would not normally be a single. "Mayday", with Ryo Kinoshita of Crystal Lake, became the opening theme of Fire Force.

    In November the band turned their yearly show, Blaredown Barriers, into a festival. The first Blare Fest was held at Port Messe Nagoya on 1 and 2 February 2020, with coldrain headlining both days above One Ok Rock, We Came as Romans, Fever 333, Crossfaith and Crystal Lake, among others.

    Weeks later the COVID-19 pandemic stopped everything. The band put their three live DVDs on YouTube for free during lockdown, and on 4 September cancelled their show at the 17,000 capacity Yokohama Arena, planned for 18 October, rather than play it with the crowd held at a distance. "What we can promise now," Masato wrote, "is that we will definitely do it someday." Live & Backstage at Blare Fest. 2020 came out on 28 October instead. When shows came back in 2021, they toured Japan on a "Setlist Election", with fans voting on the songs for each night.

    2021 – 2024: Nonnegative and the re-recording

    "Paradise (Kill the Silence)", their first new song in two years, was released on 17 September 2021 with a twelve-venue tour of Japan behind it. In January 2022 the band flew to the United States to make their seventh album with longtime producer Michael Baskette. Sugi recorded his parts at home in Japan, having just become a father for the first time. Nonnegative was released through Warner Music Japan on 6 July 2022 and reached number 15. Alongside "Calling", "Before I Go" and "Cut Me", it carried "Bloody Power Fame", the opening theme of Netflix's Bastard!!, and a cover of No Doubt's "Don't Speak".

    Blare Fest returned on 4 and 5 February 2023 with Hoobastank, Don Broco, Wargasm and SiM on the bill, and that April coldrain supported Papa Roach and The Used across Australia. The live album 15x(5+U) Live at Yokohama Arena slipped from April to 17 May 2023 when the band could not clear the rights to "Don't Speak". When Bastard!! came back for a second season, coldrain wrote its opening again: "New Dawn", released on 2 August 2023.

    "Vengeance", the opening theme of Adult Swim's Ninja Kamui, was released on 11 February 2024. The night before, at a homecoming show at Nippon Gaishi Hall in Nagoya, the band played it live for the first time and sold it on a CD with a fifteenth anniversary re-recording of Final Destination. Demand was high enough that Final Destination (XV Re:Recorded) went digital on 26 May, and the homecoming show became their sixth live album. Ninja Kamui director Sunghoo Park had given them the brief in a single word.

    The first word he gave us, the one that really stuck, was "revenge." So we decided on the title Vengeance right away and started writing from there.

    Masato, to Black Screen Records7

    2025 – present: OPTIMIZE = OPTDEMISE

    On 18 March 2025 coldrain announced a worldwide deal with the German label Century Media Records, and released "INCOMPLETE" two days later. They played Germany that August, including Summer Breeze, Open Flair and Reload, then toured Europe as headliners from Manchester on 22 November to Frankfurt on 13 December. In between came their fifth EP, OPTIMIZE, on 24 October 2025, carrying the singles "CHASING SHADOWS" and "FREE FALL", plus dates in Taipei and Hong Kong. Blare Fest 2026 brought One Ok Rock, SiM, Maximum the Hormone and Memphis May Fire to Nagoya.

    On 17 July 2026 the band released "EX-HUMANITY" and announced their eighth album, OPTIMIZE = OPTDEMISE, a sequel to the EP, due on 25 September 2026. It is their first album since Vena to get a full worldwide release. The record takes aim at a world where the rise of AI has left people confused about what being human means.

    'Ex-Humanity' is a song built on contrast. Musically, it's energetic, powerful and alive. Lyrically, it's intentionally simple, static and full of despair. That contrast reflects the tension between what it means to be human and what we could become if we lose the things that make us who we are.

    Masato, via Century Media8

    A second single, "POISON", followed on 28 August with Bert McCracken of The Used, a singer the band count among their all-time favourites. The same month, coldrain are opening for Electric Callboy on six Australian arena dates alongside Ice Nine Kills.1

    'POISON' is a song about love and desire, about how something, or someone, you love can become so addictive that it's easy to lose yourself in it.

    The band, on "POISON"9

    Artistry

    Musical style

    coldrain's music has been described as post-hardcore, metalcore, alternative metal, alternative rock, nu metal, hard rock and electronic rock, with punk rock and screamo audible in the early material. The first releases leaned towards alternative rock and post-hardcore, over guitar parts that owed a lot to classic metalcore and thrash riffing. By The Revelation the sound had settled into a mix of post-hardcore and metalcore, and critics have compared the band to My Chemical Romance, Pay Money to My Pain, Asking Alexandria, Bullet for My Valentine and A Day to Remember.

    Masato puts the big choruses down to the Japanese pop the members grew up with:

    We always wanted the melodic aspect plus the heavy music. It's cool because I think the Japanese influence towards pop music and the Western-style heavy music is what builds our sound.

    Masato, to Mixdown6

    Y.K.C. is the main composer and writes most songs with Masato. Asked how he approaches it, he said: "It is essential not to forget 'why a song is cool', the song itself needs to have holding power, and only then is there meaning. That's what I try not to forget when composing."1

    Influences

    The band came to heavy music through the nu metal boom of the late 1990s. "When we started out we were all big nu-metal fans," Masato told Mixdown.6 He grew up on his older brother's pop and R&B, Michael Jackson and Mariah Carey, next to Japanese acts such as Hikaru Utada and L'Arc-en-Ciel, until a family trip to the United States in 1999 put Limp Bizkit and Korn in front of him on MTV.

    He has named Linkin Park and Incubus as the bands that made him want to make music, and Chester Bennington and Brandon Boyd as his biggest influences as a singer. He also cites Hoobastank, Papa Roach, Metallica, Pearl Jam, The Used, Silverchair and Slipknot, and has said coldrain would not exist without Sevendust.2

    When I was 15, I visited my grandparents in the US and I went to see Nickelback. Sevendust were supporting them. They just came out jumping around the stage with these heavy, chugging riffs. It was one of the most powerful shows I'd ever seen at the time. That low end just felt good.

    Masato, to Louder4

    RxYxO has named J, Rikiji, Tim Commerford, Vinnie Hornsby and Sam Rivers as his favourite bassists.

    Language and scene

    Singing in English was rare for a Japanese band when coldrain started. For Masato it was simply more natural: his mother, an English teacher, taught him the language as a child, and he spent his summers in the United States. The band have always printed translated lyrics in their CDs and put subtitles on their videos, but the choice has cost them.

    For anime songs especially, people would say, "We'd love to use your song, but it has to be in Japanese." There were cases where we missed out on tie-in deals because our lyrics weren't in Japanese. [...] But in the end, I can only write in English. And that became part of what coldrain is: a Japanese band that sings entirely in English.

    Masato, to Black Screen Records7

    It also left them without an obvious home. They toured with Japanese punk bands without being part of that scene, and never belonged to J-rock, visual kei or pop.

    I think we've taken kids from every other place and connected them together. So, in some ways, we've made a new scene in Japan and I'm hoping it grows.

    Masato, to Louder3

    Band members

    The same five people have been in coldrain since 17 April 2007.

    Former touring musicians

    Katsuma took several months away in 2012. Three drummers from friends' bands filled in until he returned that November.

    Discography

    coldrain have released seven studio albums, a re-recorded album, six EPs, three maxi-singles, six live albums and two demos, along with around forty digital singles. The eighth album is due in September 2026.

        In other media

        A good part of the catalogue reached people through anime, games and television before it reached them on record. Masato used to think of anime songs as something apart from real artist music, and changed his mind: "since anime itself lasts forever, the music tied to it also gets remembered."7
